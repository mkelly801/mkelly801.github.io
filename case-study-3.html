Case Study #3 - Exploratory

Utilizing Deep User Knowledge: Identifying Where AI Agents Can Provide the Most Value

Led exploratory research that identified where automation could have the most impact in work order management, directly shaping the design of 4 AI agents approved for development.

Timeline

12-Week Contract (April - Q2 End)

Scale

100+ Accounts, ~5,000 Facility Managers & Coordinators

Role

Lead UX Researcher

Team

Consultant Team, Product Director

The Business Problem

CBRE employees were spending up to 40% of their time actively managing work orders. This significant time investment in manual work order management was limiting the ability of Facility Managers to focus on higher-value strategic tasks and proactive maintenance.

Goals & My Role

As a team: Build AI agents to increase efficiency (as measured by time on task) of internal workers and outcomes for clients (as measured by work order time).

As a researcher: Identify the ripest opportunities for automation within the work order flow to increase usability (as measured by UEM). I served as Lead UX Researcher for IFM Hub, including the Work Order domain, and managed the consultant team for research activities on this project.

Research Questions

Review of Past Research:

  • Where do CBRE employees spend most of their time when managing work orders?
  • What steps are duplicative or more manual than they could be?
  • What pain points arise because of these inefficiencies?

Semi-Structured Interviews:

  • What steps in the work order management process do users spend the most time on?
  • How much time do they spend on those steps?
  • What information do users need to act and what actions do they take to progress a work order?

Why These Methods

I started with a review of past research because the consultant team's planned research was duplicating work that had already been done internally. By surfacing what was already known, I prevented redundant effort and identified specific gaps that new interviews needed to fill. This let me focus the semi-structured interviews on deeper questions about time allocation and decision-making that past research hadn't covered.

R&D Activities

April - Begin Analysis: Consultant team onboarded, research kickoff occurs, review of past research and interviews start.

Q2 End - Share Findings: Research completed and findings shared, consultant team formally presents agent proposal, agents are approved by leadership.

Q3 - Agent Development: Internal agent development begins with a goal of release by end of Q3.

Key Findings

Research identified tracking and updating the work order as the step in which there was the most opportunity for agentic assistance.

Key Pain Points:

  • Lack of awareness of work order priority and action-triggering information.
  • Lack of visibility into workflow bottlenecks across the work order lifecycle.
  • Significant FM time is spent on email coordination of work orders between multiple parties.

4 AI Agents Proposed:

  • Auditor Agent: Flags SLA risks and bottlenecks
  • Actor Agent: Resolves stuck tickets and corrects priorities
  • Notifier Agent: Sends targeted alerts
  • Quality Agent: Detects patterns and anomalies

Impact & Measurement

4 AI agents proposed and approved by leadership, with development beginning in Q3. Research findings directly shaped the design and scope of each agent, defining what each agent would do and how it would address the specific pain points uncovered in the research.

Success will be measured across four dimensions: Average Work Order Time, Time on Task, UEM Score, and CSAT Scores.

Key Challenges & Reflections

Lack of Product Analytics: Product analytics capabilities matured throughout the year but were spotty before Q3 as events were being set up.

Initial Confusion on Research Lead: The consultant team was brought on to do everything, but I took lead on research as their planned research was a duplication of past internal research. This experience reinforced the importance of establishing research ownership and scope early in cross-team engagements, before work begins rather than after overlap is discovered.
