Case Study #1 - Exploratory & Evaluative

Guiding Product Strategy: Driving New Domain Development Through Exploratory & Evaluative User Research

Led discovery and evaluation research across 3 new finance domains, unblocking 4 account transitions and delivering 14 actioned insights to the product roadmap.

Timeline

Q1 - Q3 (2 Quarters)

Scale

3 New Domains, 100+ Accounts, 20,000+ Users

Role

Lead UX Researcher

Team

Principal UX Designer, Principal PM, 5 PMs, Business Partners

The Business Problem

Lack of integrated, internal finance capabilities was leading to accounts refusing to transition to the platform and causing unnecessary spend. Pre-transition accounts called out that an unintegrated finance process was a blocker for transition to the new platform, with users spending too much time bouncing between products. Additionally, reliance on third-party finance products increased long-term spend and reduced control CBRE had to meet its clients' unique needs.

Goals & My Role

As a team: Build a more centralized finance process within IFM Hub and ensure that account teams are satisfied, as measured by account transitions.

As a researcher: Ensure that inefficient and duplicative processes are minimized so that users are more satisfied (measured by UEM) and work is being completed more efficiently. I was responsible for discovery research into new finance domains to identify user needs and define user stories, and for evaluative research of designs to ensure user needs are met and flows are usable.

Research Questions

Discovery:

  • What do users need to be able to do across processes?
  • How have other CMMS platforms implemented a centralized finance process?
  • What does our current internal process look like and where are the pain points?
  • How might we capitalize on the strong points of other platforms while improving on the pain points?

Evaluation:

  • Do the planned capabilities fulfil user needs?
  • Do the users have the info they need to act effectively?
  • Are the primary user flows efficient and intuitive?

Why These Methods

There was no documented current-state process for finance workflows, and users were working across multiple tools and systems. I chose interviews with journey mapping to build that understanding from scratch and capture the full cross-tool picture. Comparative analysis was critical because we were building new capabilities and needed to understand how competing CMMS platforms had solved similar problems. This combination let us identify both what users needed and where existing solutions fell short, giving the team a foundation to design against.

Research Activities

Q1 - Discovery: Interviews with journey mapping of current process, comparative analysis, UEM review of current pain points.

Q2 - Evaluation: 2 rounds of concept testing, 1 round of task-based usability testing, iteration as necessary. All 3 domains were developed in parallel and released together.

Q3 - Release: User acceptance testing, quarterly monitoring of UEM.

Key Discovery Insight

In current state, only 2 quotes could be added to any given work order, and a new work order had to be created for a quote from a second provider. This made comparing and approving quotes much more challenging. This was delivered via journey map and slide deck. This insight became a key user story that the product team built the new quoting flow around.

Key Evaluation Insights

Concept Testing: Overall, feedback across flows was positive. Users called out several data points they typically use to filter by that were not included in the prototype: priority, site, asset name.

Usability Testing: The only task that was failed by a majority of users was comparing quotes. This was due to the "compare" button not being intuitive to users. Replacing the compare button in the UI with something more intuitive would alleviate this issue.

Impact

4 accounts unblocked. These accounts now feel comfortable transitioning to IFM Hub, progressing towards the high-level goal of transitioning all SI7 accounts.

14 insights actioned by product. From discovery through evaluation, UX research had a quantifiable impact on the product roadmap and release state of the new domains.

6.1-point increase in UEM. From Q2 to Q3, there was a 6.1-point jump in Total UEM. Part of the cause of this jump may be due to improved integration and finance flow experience.

Key Challenges & Reflections

No Budget for Scaled Usability Testing: This resulted in the inability to capture enough time on task data to be confident in declaring that we saw a significant improvement had we done 2 rounds of usability testing.

Design Being Built During Testing: This resulted in the need to work iteratively and take extra care to track what concepts users had seen during concept testing. In future projects, I would push harder for designs to be more complete before beginning evaluation to reduce this complexity and improve data quality.

Complex Processes: I had not worked on finance-related products before this work, so I needed to quickly get up to speed on complex topics so I could properly empathize and communicate with end users.
