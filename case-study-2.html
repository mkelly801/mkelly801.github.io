Case Study #2 - Evaluative

Creating a Feedback Loop: The Cycle of Measuring & Improving Usability in Enterprise Products

Used existing UEM data to pinpoint a usability issue in the self-service request flow, leading to an 18.4% reduction in call center rate and a 7% UEM score increase.

Timeline

February - Q2+ (Ongoing Monitoring)

Scale

20,000 Requests/Month, 100+ Accounts, 10,000+ Users

Role

Lead UX Researcher

Team

UX Designer, Product Manager, Business Partners

The Business Problem

By the end of 2024, 50% of service requests were being input via calls to CBRE call centers. Such a high rate of call center calls was resulting in call center employees spending less time on emergency or highly complex requests, instead spending much of their time on straightforward requests that should be able to be input via self-service.

Goals & My Role

As a team: Make improvements to the Self-Service Request flow so that calls to the Call Center go down, as measured by call center rate.

As a researcher: Identify if there is an underlying issue causing users to use the Self-Service Request less than expected. Determine what design improvements can be made to increase usability scores and usage of the Self-Service flow. I served as Lead UX Researcher for IFM Hub and owner of the quarterly User Experience Metric.

Baseline Metrics

  • 50% Call Center Rate of total requests (20,000 requests a month)
  • 64.7 UEM Score for the Self-Service module in Q1 (70 is the score determined to be "good")
  • N/A Time on Task (no product analytics available in Q1 2025)

Research Questions

Qualitative Analysis of UEM Comments:

  • What themes or pain points are surfacing in the UEM comments?
  • For each theme or pain point, how many users are mentioning it compared to other themes?

Testing of Problematic Flow:

  • What information do they need to efficiently input a service request?
  • What is preventing them from accomplishing this?
  • What is causing them to drop-off?

Why These Methods

I started with qualitative analysis of UEM comments because that data already existed and could quickly point to where the problem was. This was both efficient given the tight timeline and strategic: it let me narrow focus before investing time in new research. Once the UEM analysis surfaced the location selector as the likely culprit, I moved to concept testing to validate potential design fixes with users before committing to development.

Research Activities

February - Begin Analysis: Q1 (Baseline) UEM released in January; business problem identified in February.

March - Enhancement Release: Qualitative analysis of UEM responses, concept testing, design improvements and release.

Q2+ - Monitor: Using the rest of the year to monitor and complete retrospective on success of project.

Key Finding

The location selector within the request flow was unforgiving when attempting to input locations. This was uncovered in UEM qualitative analysis and confirmed in concept testing.

  • Many users weren't saving their default location.
  • When trying to change the room or area for a location, users couldn't click back to the previous hierarchy step but rather had to retype the entire location.
  • Search results for a location were showing each hierarchy level when most users were always at just one building, obfuscating key information.

Insights led to a Jira ticket for design improvements and the design improvements were picked up by dev and released by the end of Q1.

Impact

18.4% reduction in call center request rate.

7% increase in total UEM score for the Self-Service module.

24 seconds time on task to select a location (baseline established).

Key Challenges & Reflections

Lack of Product Analytics: Product analytics capabilities matured throughout the year but were spotty before Q3 as events were being set up.

Quick Turnaround: A tight timeline limited research's ability to do more rigorous testing than concept testing. In other scenarios, I might push for task-based usability testing to establish reliable time on task data, but in this instance the change was relatively clear. I chose to forego additional rigor for the sake of getting the enhancement into production quicker and at lower cost.

Inability to Prove Causality: While we do see metrics like call center rate and UEM score improve, I did not test these designs in a controlled experiment, so I have no way of truly identifying that these design improvements directly caused the improvements in metrics. The infrastructure for causal testing was not set up at the time and would have taken much longer to develop than the project timeline allowed.
